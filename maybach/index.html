What to say

The call, start to finish

You are not the renter here, your friend’s parent is. So you are calling to check the car is free and find out what they need. Keep it simple and do not oversell it.

“Hi, I’m checking on the Maybach GLS600 for Saturday October 24, self-drive for the day. Is it open that date?”

Lead with the date. If the car is already booked nothing else matters and you both save ten minutes. Say GLS600, not just “the Maybach” — they have an S580 too and you will end up talking about the wrong car.

“Is the $1,200 a flat 24 hours, and does a Saturday cost more?”

Then: what the mileage cap is and what each extra mile costs. A full night of pickups, dinner and drop-offs eats 100 miles quickly, and buying extra miles up front is almost always cheaper than paying overage after.

“The driver would be my friend’s dad. He’s over 25 with his own full coverage. What do you need from him to start the application?”

Say this plainly rather than working around it. It is completely normal to sort out details for an adult who will sign, and being straight about it gets you taken seriously. If they ask whether you will be driving, the answer is no.

“One more thing. Could you send a photo of the Off-road Assistant screen with Recovery Mode showing?”

The whole reason you want this car. Ask for it as a favour, not a demand — two minutes of their time. Call it E-Active Body Control recovery mode. Saying “bounce mode” gets you a blank look, and asking whether you’re allowed to use it is a conversation for after they know you’re a real booking, not in the first minute.

Don’t: open with the bouncing. It is the first thing a rental company hears as “this kid is going to abuse my $200,000 car.” Get the booking real first, then raise recovery mode and ask for written permission before the day.

Read before using it

It may void the damage waiver

No rental agreement names this feature. The problem is what it requires: OFF-ROAD drive program with the suspension raised. Exotic contracts routinely list off-road use and areas not intended for normal vehicle traffic under Prohibited Use, and violating those clauses terminates the rental and voids all liability protection, damage waiver included.

The physics back up the worry. On sand, the sand absorbs the impact. On concrete, that energy goes back into the air struts, the 48‑volt actuators and the bushings. Mercedes built in an automatic shutdown when the system detects a hard underbody strike, which tells you what they were designing against. Suspension work on a Maybach GLS is dealer-only and runs into the thousands, and tyres, wheels and underbody are the exact categories waivers exclude anyway.

Get it in writing. An email saying “we authorize use of the vehicle’s E-Active Body Control recovery mode” is the whole ballgame. Factory-installed is not implied permission.

Before money changes hands

Four questions

“Is the GLS600 available self-drive on Saturday the 24th, or is it held for chauffeur work?”

The one ambiguity in their listing. The car appears in the self-drive inventory and on the chauffeur page, and an earlier read of their site concluded wrongly that it was driver-only. Settle it in the first minute.

“Walk to the car, put it in drive with OFF-ROAD selected, and send me a photo of the Off-road Assistant screen.”

The best verification there is. You want Recovery Mode visible and not greyed out. It proves the feature works, the car is the right model, and the system is not in a fault state. Two minutes of their time.

“What does the mileage overage actually run on this car?”

Their policy is 100 miles a day then $3 to $7 per mile depending on the vehicle, and a Maybach sits at the top of that band. At $7 a mile an extra 40 miles is $280. Ask what it costs to buy more miles up front instead.

“Does the damage waiver also cover diminished value and loss of use?”

The question that actually caps the downside. A waiver covering repair cost alone still leaves six figures exposed — see below. Ask it in the same breath as the recovery-mode permission, because a waiver you have voided protects nothing.

Show this to the parent

The deposit is $1,200. The exposure is $300,000.

A refundable hold equal to one day’s rate reads as “my downside is $1,200.” It is not a cap on anything.

$200kthe car’s actual cash value
$40–80kdiminished value, owed even after a perfect repair

Those stack. Total the car and the realistic bill is $280,000 to $340,000 before towing and admin fees. Texas courts let rental companies recover loss of use, and a fleet with one Maybach argues persuasively that nothing substitutes for it.

The credit card will not save them. Visa Signature and Infinite exclude any car over $75,000 MSRP and name Maybach on the excluded-exotics list — that benefit pays zero. Chase Sapphire Reserve is the best mainstream card here and caps at $75,000. Amex’s free benefit caps at $50,000; its paid Premium Car Rental Protection tops out near $100,000. That is the ceiling across the entire card market, and all of them exclude diminished value by name.

Their liability coverage is the wrong coverage. Part A excludes damage to property “in the care of” the insured. The Maybach is in their care, so liability limits are irrelevant to the car itself. Only comprehensive and collision can reach it, and whether that extends to a $200,000 exotic lives in their carrier’s filed form — the only acceptable answer is one in writing citing the form number.

The absolute rule: you cannot touch the wheel. Not to move it in a lot, not for a photo, not for ten feet. An unlisted driver voids everything above instantly and makes the parent personally liable for the full value of the car.

The other risk

Injuring someone else’s kid

Texas minimum financial responsibility is 30/60/25. One serious injury to a teenager with surgery and rehab clears $60,000 immediately, and the driver is personally liable for everything above it. A minor’s limitations period generally does not start running until they turn 18, so the exposure stays open for years.

Before the 24th: raise bodily injury to 250/500 and confirm a $1M–$2M umbrella covering non-owned and rented autos. Most do, but it has to be confirmed rather than assumed. Text each parent naming who is driving, what car, where and when, and get a yes back — not a legal shield, but it prevents the much worse conversation where a parent says they never agreed.
