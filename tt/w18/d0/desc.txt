the history behind the casino floor

1. The Flamingo nearly killed the idea — Bugsy Siegel's 1946 resort went massively over budget and flopped at first. It became the template for every resort after.
2. Corporations replaced the mob in the 60s — Howard Hughes bought up casinos with cash, which let public companies enter legally. That's when Vegas got its polish.
3. Blackjack got popular from a math book — Ed Thorp's 'Beat the Dealer' in 1962 proved the game was beatable. Casinos panicked, changed rules, then realized it sold more tables.
4. Slots started as a bar novelty — The first machine paid out in drinks and cigars, not money. It was a distraction, not the main revenue engine it is now.
5. The house edge was always the point — Every game ever added to a floor was chosen for one reason: the math had to favor the building. Nothing about it is accidental.
6. Vegas was legalized out of desperation — Nevada legalized gambling in 1931 during the Depression to generate tax revenue. The whole city is a budget fix that worked.
