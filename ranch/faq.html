Questions

Including the ones that make a share sound like a worse idea. If something here puts you off, it was going to put you off in month four anyway.

What am I actually buying?

A share of one live Black Angus animal, raised here in Argyle. You are not buying meat by the pound. You get a bill of sale for your share before the animal is harvested, and once it is processed, the beef from your share is yours. This is how the law works for a ranch our size, and it is also simply a more honest description of what is happening.

How much beef do I get?

It depends on the animal. Ours finish at around 1,300 lb live, which comes back as roughly 460 to 540 lb of packaged beef for a whole animal — so about 230 to 270 lb for a half, 115 to 135 lb for a quarter, and 58 to 67 lb for an eighth. We publish ranges rather than one number because the real figure depends on the individual animal, and we would rather under-promise it.

How much of it is ground beef?

Between 38% and 50%. That is true of every beef share anywhere; it is simply how an animal is built. The rib and loin steaks everyone pictures are about 16% of the yield. If your household only eats ribeye and filet, buying those cuts individually will cost you less than a share will.

What freezer do I need?

Roughly one cubic foot for every 25 to 35 lb of packaged beef. An eighth needs about 2 to 2.5 cu ft and will fit in an empty kitchen freezer. A quarter needs 4 to 7, a half needs 8 to 10 in a chest freezer, and a whole animal needs 15 to 20 and often two appliances. A freezer is a real cost that is not in the share price, so factor it in.

How long does it keep?

Steaks and roasts hold excellent quality for about 4 to 12 months at 0°F, and ground beef for 3 to 4. Food held constantly at 0°F is safe indefinitely — those are quality guidelines, not safety limits. Vacuum-sealed beef holds at the long end of the range. Practically: plan to eat a half share inside a year.

Can I choose my cuts?

Yes, and we will go through the sheet with you. What you cannot do is have everything at once, because the choices trade off against each other. A T-bone is a New York strip and a filet still joined to the bone, so you can have 20 T-bones or you can have strips and filets separately. Same with a bone-in ribeye versus a boneless ribeye plus a rib roast. There is one animal and the cuts have to come out of it.

When do I get it?

On the animal’s schedule, not on demand. Cattle finish when they finish. We will give you an expected month when you reserve, confirm the processing date a few weeks out, and the beef usually comes back two to three weeks after that. If you need beef next Tuesday, a share is the wrong thing to buy.

Does it taste different?

Less than you might expect, and probably in your favour. Our cattle are raised on grass and finished on grain, which is how most of the beef people actually enjoy eating is produced. The grain finish is what lays down marbling, and marbling is what makes a steak tender and forgiving if you leave it on a minute too long. If you have tried fully grass-finished beef and found it lean or a little gamey, this is not that. What you will notice instead is consistency: it is one animal rather than a blend, so the whole share eats the same way.

Is this cheaper than the grocery store?

No, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Supermarket beef is cheap — the US Bureau of Labor Statistics put the national average for ground beef at $6.79 a pound in September 2026 — and a share here works out between about $12.40 and $14.40 a pound depending on the size you take. If price per pound is the only thing you are weighing, the grocery store wins and it is not close. What you get instead is a known animal from one place you can drive to, ground beef that came from that animal and no other, and a freezer you stop thinking about for a year. That is worth a premium to some people and nothing at all to others. Work out which you are before you spend $3,299.

How does this actually compare to supermarket beef?

Three parts, and only one of them is a win for us. What is genuinely different: your ground beef comes from your animal and no other one. Commercial ground beef is made by combining trim from many carcasses, and a single batch can contain meat from several hundred different cattle. Ours is one. You also know the date it was processed and the field it stood in, which nobody behind a supermarket counter can tell you. What is not different: safety. We are not going to tell you shop beef is full of preservatives, because it is not, or that ours passes more checks, because it does not. Beef sold in a shop is USDA-inspected. Ours is processed under the Texas custom exemption, which means it is inspected less, not more. That is a real trade and you should make it knowingly. What is a wash: freshness. Ours is frozen within days of processing; shop beef is chilled and has usually sat in vacuum for weeks. Different, not better.

What if my freezer dies?

It is worth saying out loud: a half share is 200-odd pounds of beef in one appliance. A cheap freezer alarm is about $20 and is the best money you will spend on this. Check whether your homeowner’s policy covers freezer contents.

Can I visit?

Yes. That is most of the reason the ranch is set up this way. Get in touch and we will find a time — it works best around feeding, and better on a weekend.

Is a share an investment?

No. A share buys you an animal and the beef from it. It carries no financial return, no profit share, no appreciation, no resale value and no buy-back, and shares are not transferable. It is a purchase of food.

What happens if something happens to the animal?

Livestock is livestock. If an animal you have a share in is lost or has to be pulled, we move your share to another animal of the same age and finish, or refund you in full. Your choice, and we will tell you as soon as we know.

  • Terms and disclosures

When you reserve a share, you are purchasing an ownership interest in one specific, identified live Black Angus animal raised at our ranch in Argyle, Texas. You are not buying meat, and you are not making an investment.

You will receive a bill of sale conveying your ownership interest before the animal is harvested, together with a written animal-share agreement authorising us to board and care for your animal and to arrange its processing on your behalf. Your share is sold under the animal share exemption in Section 433.0065 of the Texas Health and Safety Code and 25 Texas Administrative Code Section 221.14(d).

Your animal is processed at a licensed Texas custom-exempt facility. Because the animal is processed for its owners rather than for sale, it is not inspected, and the law requires every package to be marked “Not for Sale.”

THIS MEAT HAS NOT BEEN INSPECTED BY THE TEXAS DEPARTMENT OF STATE HEALTH SERVICES.
